Repairs & Small Jobs

The Rate Card

Minimum Visit
$150

Covers getting to you, getting set up, and the first hour of work.

After the First Hour
$65/hr

Straight hourly, billed in half-hour steps. Materials at cost on top.

Full Day  (8 hours)
$550

A full 8-hour day on site. The same 8 hours billed hourly would run $605 — past 8 hours, it goes back to $65/hr.

There's no trip fee on top of this. The $150 minimum only shows up when the job is small — if the work adds up to more than $150, that's simply what you pay. No surprises, no separate charge for showing up.

Got more than one thing? Tell Bob the whole list. Bundling several jobs into one visit almost always costs you less than calling him out three separate times — and he's happy to knock out a whole list in one trip.

Materials are billed at cost. Bob doesn't mark up parts. If he picks up $40 of lumber, you pay $40.

What that rate already covers

People compare Bob's hourly to what they'd pay a guy for an hour of labor. It isn't the same thing. Everything below is already in the number:

  • His truck and his gas — both directions, every trip
  • Every tool he owns — you're not renting a thing, and he's not showing up needing to borrow yours
  • A parts run if the job turns out to need something he doesn't have on the truck
  • Cleanup when he's done — the space gets left the way he found it
  • Hauling off what's left — the old door, the torn-out trim, the scrap, the packaging. It leaves in his truck, not in your garage

The one exception: a full tear-out that fills a truck bed — a whole deck, a gutted bathroom — is its own line item. He'll tell you that up front, not after.

Why $150 for a small job?

Because a "twenty-minute fix" is never twenty minutes for Bob. It's the drive out, the drive back, unloading tools, setting up, cleaning up, packing up, and hauling off what came out — usually the better part of two hours of his day for one stop.

The minimum is what makes it possible for him to say yes to small work at all instead of quietly not returning the call. That's the honest reason, and it's why bundling is always the better deal for you.

Why he comes out every time

Bob's a one-man operation, and he'd rather see it than guess at it. A price given without looking is a price that can change once he's actually there — and nobody wants that conversation.

Coming out to look is part of the visit, not an extra trip. That's what makes the number he gives you one you can count on.

What Bob Doesn't Do

Electrical panel work, HVAC, plumbing that requires a licensed plumber, and roofing. He's not going to take your money for something that needs a licensed trade.

If that's what you need, tell him anyway — after this many years around Louisville, he usually knows someone worth calling.
